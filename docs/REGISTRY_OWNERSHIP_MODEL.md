# CivicSync v2 — Title, Occupancy and Membership

**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 26 Aug 2026 · **Supersedes** the two-table
`units → occupancies` sketch in `PRODUCTION_PLAN.md` §4.1 and
`ARCHITECTURE_DDD.md` §1.
**Module:** `registry` · **Packet:** 05

## The correction

The earlier model had `Occupancy` carry a `relationship` of owner / co-owner / tenant.
That conflates two independent facts and cannot express what actually happens in an
Indian housing society.

**Ownership and occupancy are orthogonal.** An owner may not live in the flat. A tenant
occupies without owning. A flat may have four joint owners and one resident family. A
sale changes ownership without anyone moving. A tenant moving out changes occupancy
without title changing hands.

And there is a third fact neither captures: **association membership**. In a registered
cooperative housing society, "member" is a *legal* status attached to a share
certificate, not to title or to residence — which is precisely why the prototype called
everyone a "member" and why that word became so overloaded. Membership carries voting
rights and committee eligibility, and its rules are set by the society's bye-laws.

So: **three independent time-bounded relationships between a Party and a Unit.**

| Concern | Question it answers | Governs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Title** (ownership) | Who legally owns this unit, in what shares? | Financial liability, sale, share certificate |
| **Occupancy** | Who physically lives here right now? | Gate passes, visitors, parking, utilities, resident portal |
| **Association membership** | Who has standing in the society? | Voting, AGM, committee eligibility, correspondence |

They overlap in the common case — a self-occupying sole owner is all three — but the
model must not assume it.

---

## 1. Structure

```
Unit
 ├── UnitTitle          the ownership set over time      → who owns it
 │     └── Ownership    party × share × validity
 ├── Occupancy          the household over time          → who lives there
 │     ├── Occupant     party × relationship-to-household
 │     └── Tenancy      lease terms, when tenanted
 └── AssociationMembership  party × membership type × validity
```

Plus two process aggregates that manage the transitions you called handshakes:

```
OwnershipTransfer   sale / gift / inheritance / partition
TenancyOnboarding   tenant move-in and move-out
```

## 2. Title

### Holding types

Indian co-ownership takes forms that a single `share` column cannot express:

| Holding type | Shares | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sole` | One owner, 100% | Most common |
| `joint` | Two or more, **shares undefined** | Very common — spouses holding "jointly". Survivorship applies; shares are not separately alienable |
| `tenancy_in_common` | Two or more, defined fractional shares | Each share is separately transferable and inheritable |

`share_fraction` is **nullable**, and must be null for `joint` and present for
`tenancy_in_common`. Forcing a 50/50 onto joint owners is a factual error that will
eventually appear in a legal document generated by your system.

### Schema

```sql
CREATE TABLE ownerships (
    id             uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
    org_id         uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES control.tenants(org_id),
    unit_id        uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES units(id),
    party_id       uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES parties(id),

    holding_type   text NOT NULL
                     CHECK (holding_type IN ('sole','joint','tenancy_in_common')),
    share_fraction numeric(9,6)
                     CHECK (share_fraction > 0 AND share_fraction <= 1),
    is_primary     boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT false,   -- correspondence + membership holder

    acquired_on    date NOT NULL,
    acquisition_type_id uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES acquisition_types(id),  -- master data
    disposed_on    date,
    disposal_transfer_id uuid REFERENCES ownership_transfers(id),
    acquisition_transfer_id uuid REFERENCES ownership_transfers(id),

    validity       daterange GENERATED ALWAYS AS
                     (daterange(acquired_on, disposed_on, '[)')) STORED,

    -- spine
    created_at timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now(), created_by uuid,
    updated_at timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now(), updated_by uuid,
    deleted_at timestamptz, version int NOT NULL DEFAULT 1,

    CONSTRAINT share_matches_holding CHECK (
        (holding_type = 'tenancy_in_common' AND share_fraction IS NOT NULL) OR
        (holding_type IN ('sole','joint')   AND share_fraction IS NULL)
    ),

    -- the same person cannot hold two overlapping interests in one unit
    EXCLUDE USING gist (
        org_id WITH =, unit_id WITH =, party_id WITH =, validity WITH &&
    ) WHERE (deleted_at IS NULL)
);

CREATE INDEX ON ownerships (org_id, unit_id, acquired_on DESC);
CREATE INDEX ON ownerships (org_id, party_id) WHERE disposed_on IS NULL;
```

`acquisition_types` is master data: purchase, allotment, gift, inheritance, partition,
court decree, builder handover.

The `EXCLUDE` constraint needs `btree_gist`. It is worth it — **temporal overlap bugs
are the single most common defect in this kind of model**, and PostgreSQL will refuse
them at the database level rather than relying on application code that will eventually
have a race.

### Invariants — enforced in the domain, verified nightly

Some of these span rows, so they belong to the **UnitTitle aggregate** (root: Unit), not
to a single `Ownership` row:

1. At any date on or after first registration, a unit has **at least one** current owner
2. For `tenancy_in_common`, current shares **sum to exactly 1.000000**
3. Exactly **one** current owner has `is_primary = true`
4. All current owners share the same `holding_type`
5. `disposed_on` is null or after `acquired_on`
6. No gaps in the ownership timeline once the unit is registered

Constraints 1, 2, 3 and 6 cannot be expressed as row constraints. They are enforced by
the aggregate on every mutation, and re-verified by a nightly integrity job that raises
an admin alert rather than failing silently. Belt and braces, because a broken title
record surfaces years later during a sale.

## 3. Occupancy

```sql
CREATE TABLE occupancies (
    id              uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
    org_id          uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES control.tenants(org_id),
    unit_id         uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES units(id),

    occupancy_type  text NOT NULL
                      CHECK (occupancy_type IN
                        ('owner_occupied','tenanted','vacant','under_renovation','locked')),
    occupied_from   date NOT NULL,
    occupied_to     date,
    validity        daterange GENERATED ALWAYS AS
                      (daterange(occupied_from, occupied_to, '[)')) STORED,

    primary_occupant_party_id uuid REFERENCES parties(id),
    tenancy_id      uuid REFERENCES tenancies(id),
    handover_notes  text NOT NULL DEFAULT '',

    created_at timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now(), created_by uuid,
    updated_at timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now(), updated_by uuid,
    deleted_at timestamptz, version int NOT NULL DEFAULT 1,

    CONSTRAINT tenancy_required CHECK (
        (occupancy_type = 'tenanted' AND tenancy_id IS NOT NULL) OR
        (occupancy_type <> 'tenanted' AND tenancy_id IS NULL)
    ),
    CONSTRAINT occupant_required CHECK (
        (occupancy_type IN ('vacant','under_renovation','locked')
         AND primary_occupant_party_id IS NULL)
        OR primary_occupant_party_id IS NOT NULL
    ),

    -- a unit has at most one occupancy state at any moment
    EXCLUDE USING gist (org_id WITH =, unit_id WITH =, validity WITH &&)
        WHERE (deleted_at IS NULL)
);

CREATE TABLE occupants (
    id            uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
    org_id        uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES control.tenants(org_id),
    occupancy_id  uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES occupancies(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
    party_id      uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES parties(id),
    relation_id   uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES household_relations(id),  -- master data
    moved_in_on   date NOT NULL,
    moved_out_on  date,
    is_minor      boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT false,
    portal_access boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT false,
    -- spine …
    UNIQUE (org_id, occupancy_id, party_id, moved_in_on)
);
```

`household_relations` is master data: self, spouse, child, parent, sibling, relative,
domestic staff, driver, paying guest, other.

**Vacancy is a first-class occupancy state, not an absence of rows.** That is what makes
"was this flat occupied in March 2025?" answerable, and it is what a vacancy-rebate rule
in the fee schedule needs to read.

### Tenancy

```sql
CREATE TABLE tenancies (
    id                    uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
    org_id                uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES control.tenants(org_id),
    unit_id               uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES units(id),
    tenant_party_id       uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES parties(id),

    lease_start           date NOT NULL,
    lease_end             date NOT NULL,
    notice_period_days    int,
    monthly_rent          numeric(14,2),
    security_deposit      numeric(14,2),
    currency_code         char(3) NOT NULL DEFAULT 'INR',

    agreement_registered  boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT false,
    agreement_document_id uuid REFERENCES documents(id),
    police_verification_status text NOT NULL DEFAULT 'not_started'
        CHECK (police_verification_status IN
               ('not_started','submitted','verified','rejected','not_applicable')),
    police_verification_document_id uuid REFERENCES documents(id),
    society_noc_document_id         uuid REFERENCES documents(id),

    status text NOT NULL DEFAULT 'draft'
        CHECK (status IN ('draft','pending_approval','active','expired','terminated')),
    terminated_on   date,
    termination_reason text,
    -- spine …
    CONSTRAINT lease_dates CHECK (lease_end > lease_start)
);
```

**Police verification is a legal requirement for tenants in most Indian cities**, and
societies are routinely held responsible for not collecting it. It is a tracked,
document-backed field with its own status — not a checkbox. Whether it is mandatory is a
society setting (`registry.require_police_verification`), because the rule varies by
state.

## 4. Association membership

```sql
CREATE TABLE association_memberships (
    id                uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT uuidv7(),
    org_id            uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES control.tenants(org_id),
    unit_id           uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES units(id),
    party_id          uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES parties(id),
    membership_type_id uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES membership_types(id),  -- master data

    member_number     text,
    share_certificate_no text,
    share_count       int,
    admitted_on       date NOT NULL,
    ceased_on         date,
    cessation_reason  text,
    validity          daterange GENERATED ALWAYS AS
                        (daterange(admitted_on, ceased_on, '[)')) STORED,
    -- spine …
    UNIQUE (org_id, share_certificate_no) WHERE share_certificate_no IS NOT NULL
);
```

`membership_types` is master data, and its defaults reflect co-operative society
bye-laws:

| Type | Typically | Voting | Committee eligible |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `primary` | The primary owner; holds the share certificate | Yes | Yes |
| `associate` | Joint owners | Only if primary is absent | Usually not |
| `nominal` | Tenants, licensees | No | No |

**Voting rights and committee eligibility are attributes of the membership type, not
hardcoded.** A society whose bye-laws differ configures its own — which is the ERP
doctrine applied to governance.

> **Naming collision to fix:** `MODULE_REGISTER.md` uses `memberships` for the RBAC
> table (user × org × role). Rename that to **`role_assignments`**. "Membership" in this
> product means association membership, and one word must not mean two things.

## 5. The handshakes

Both are `platform.workflow` instances, so their approval chains are configurable per
society.

### 5.1 Ownership transfer — sale, gift, inheritance, partition

```
initiated → dues_statement_issued → dues_settled → documents_verified
          → committee_approved → effective → completed
                              ↘ rejected / withdrawn
```

| Step | What happens | Gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Initiate | Seller or admin opens a transfer with an intended effective date and the incoming owner set | `registry.transfer.create` |
| 2. Dues statement | System generates the unit's statement as at the effective date | Automatic |
| 3. Dues settlement | Arrears cleared, **or** explicitly assumed by the buyer with written acceptance | **Blocking.** Override requires an authorised role and a recorded reason |
| 4. Documents | Sale deed / gift deed / succession certificate, buyer KYC, share transfer form | Configurable per acquisition type |
| 5. Approval | Committee approves per workflow definition | Configurable chain |
| 6. Effective | Atomic: close outgoing ownerships, open incoming, transfer share certificate, close outgoing memberships, admit new ones, revoke and grant portal access, re-evaluate occupancy | One transaction |
| 7. Emit | `registry.ownership.transferred` | Downstream reacts |

**Step 3 is the commercially important one.** In an Indian housing society, **dues attach
to the flat and follow it on sale** — that is precisely why societies issue a No Dues
Certificate before a transfer. Modelling the unit account against the *unit* rather than
the person is what makes this correct, and it is why the earlier decision to attach the
ledger to `UnitAccount` matters here.

Step 6 must be a single transaction because a half-completed transfer leaves a unit with
no owner or two conflicting owner sets. This is the one place where the "one aggregate
per transaction" rule is deliberately relaxed, and the reason is written into the code:
title has no valid intermediate state.

Occupancy is **not** automatically changed. A sold flat may keep the same tenant, or the
buyer may move in. Step 6 raises a task; a human decides.

### 5.2 Tenancy onboarding and exit

```
draft → owner_submitted → documents_verified → society_approved
      → active → notice_given → exit_inspection → cleared → ended
```

| Step | Gate |
| --- | --- |
| Owner (or agent) registers the tenant and lease terms | Must be a current owner of the unit |
| Documents: registered agreement, tenant KYC, police verification | Configurable; police verification per society setting |
| Society approval / NOC | Configurable chain |
| Activate | Closes the previous occupancy, opens a `tenanted` one, admits a `nominal` membership, grants scoped portal access |
| Exit | Notice, clearance of any occupier-liable charges, deposit settlement is *between owner and tenant* — the society records, does not hold |
| End | Closes occupancy, revokes tenant access, opens a `vacant` occupancy |

**Access scoping matters here.** A tenant sees their own dues, their unit's notices, gate
passes and amenity bookings. A tenant does not see society financials, member registers
or the owner's arrears. A non-resident owner still sees their unit's dues and documents
but has no gate-pass rights. So `role_assignments` must be **scopeable to a unit and to a
relationship**, not only to an organization. That is a Packet 02 requirement, and it
comes from this model.

## 6. Who pays what

The reason all of this matters financially.

```sql
ALTER TABLE charge_heads
    ADD COLUMN liable_party text NOT NULL DEFAULT 'owner'
        CHECK (liable_party IN ('owner','occupier','primary_owner','as_configured'));
```

| Charge | Typically liable | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maintenance, sinking fund, corpus | **Owner** | Attaches to the property; follows it on sale |
| Water, electricity by consumption | **Occupier** | Usage-based |
| Parking | Configurable | Depends on whether the slot is owned or allotted |
| Amenity booking, gate/visitor charges | **Occupier** | The user incurs it |
| Transfer / NOC fee | **Owner** | Triggered by the transfer |
| Penalty on late payment | Follows the underlying charge | |

**The unit account still holds the balance.** `liable_party` decides who the invoice is
*addressed* to and who gets chased. The society's ultimate recourse remains the unit,
which is why a No Dues Certificate is a gate on sale. Getting this distinction wrong —
attaching the balance to a person — is what makes a tenant's unpaid water bill vanish
when they move out.

Invoice gains `billed_to_party_id`, resolved at issue time from the responsibility rule
and the relationships valid on the charge date. It is stored, not recomputed, so a
reprinted invoice from 2027 still shows who it was addressed to.

## 7. Aggregates — revised

| Aggregate | Root | Contains | Key invariants |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **UnitTitle** | Unit | The unit's ownership set | Shares sum to 1; exactly one primary; no gaps; consistent holding type |
| **Occupancy** | Occupancy | Occupants, tenancy reference | No overlap per unit; tenanted implies a tenancy; vacant implies no occupant |
| **OwnershipTransfer** | OwnershipTransfer | Steps, documents, approvals | Cannot go effective with unsettled dues unless overridden and recorded; effective step is atomic |
| **TenancyOnboarding** | Tenancy | Documents, approvals | Cannot activate without required documents; only a current owner may initiate |
| **AssociationMembership** | AssociationMembership | — | One primary membership per unit at a time; share certificate unique per society |
| **Party** | Party | Contacts, identity refs | Unchanged |
| **UnitAccount** | UnitAccount | Ledger entries | Unchanged — balance follows the unit, never the person |

## 8. Migration impact

The v1 data has one row per "member" with a tower and flat number, and no way to
distinguish owner from tenant. Sidhant has confirmed the RWA will verify and correct.

Each of the 370 v1 members becomes:

| v2 record | Value | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `parties` | Name, phone, email as-is | Migrated |
| `units` | From `tower` + `flat_no` | Migrated |
| `ownerships` | `holding_type='sole'`, `is_primary=true`, `acquired_on = created_at`, `acquisition_type='legacy_import'` | **Assumed** |
| `occupancies` | `owner_occupied`, from the same date | **Assumed** |
| `occupants` | One occupant, relation `self` | **Assumed** |
| `association_memberships` | `primary`, admitted on the same date | **Assumed** |

Every assumed row carries `source_confidence = 'assumed'` and appears in a **Registry
Verification** worklist in the admin console: one screen, one unit at a time, where the
committee confirms or corrects owner versus tenant, adds joint owners, and fills in
share certificate numbers. Rows flip to `confirmed` as they are reviewed.

This turns an unknowable data-quality problem into a tracked business process with a
progress bar — which is the only honest way to handle "we do not know who actually owns
these flats." It also gives the society a visible reason to engage with the new system
early, which is worth more than the data itself.

The 9 soft-deleted members become parties with closed ownerships and occupancies, so
their history and payments survive intact.

## 9. What this unlocks

Modelling title separately is not tidiness. It is the precondition for:

- **Correct billing** when owner and occupier differ, and correct chasing when they do
- **No Dues Certificates** and clean resale — the transaction societies care most about
- **AGM and e-voting** with legally defensible eligibility, from membership type and
  share, not from a `role` column
- **Tenant compliance** — police verification, agreement expiry, renewal reminders
- **Gate passes and parking** attaching to the current occupant, not to whoever bought
  the flat in 2019
- **Owner self-service** — a landlord in Dubai managing a flat in Bengaluru, seeing dues
  and approving a new tenant, which is a real and common case
- **Inheritance and partition**, which single-owner models cannot represent at all

Every one of those is a thing an RWA will ask for, and none of them is retrofittable onto
a `member` row with a flat number.
